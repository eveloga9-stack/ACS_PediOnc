Acute Chest Syndrome

St. Jude Children's Research Hospital · Hospitalist Department

Front — Respiratory distress

First 20 minutes

!Antibiotics within ≤ 60 minutes.
1

Recognize: respiratory distress, hypoxia.

2

Obtain STAT CBC/D, retic, type & screen, chems, blood cultures, VBG, RVP, 2-view (PA & lateral) CXR. Start broad-spectrum antibiotics (ceftriaxone), ± azithromycin.

3

10–20 mL/kg bolus if hypotensive — be cautious and AVOID fluid volume overload — then reassess.

4

Notify Heme attending to discuss patient status and potential for PRBC transfusion as treatment for ACS. Escalate per the sJAWS algorithm and the Sepsis Bundle.

Back — Escalation support

When to call, and who

!No improvement after supplemental O₂ → escalate to PICU.
1

If no improvement after supplemental O₂ → escalate to the PICU team.

2

RRT triggers: hypotension, altered mental status, and/or increased work of breathing & O₂ requirement.

3

Continue antibiotics (consider escalating) and supportive care while preparing for transfer.

4

Communicate updates to the Heme attending and charge nurse.

5

Who to call: on-call Heme attending · PICU · Hospitalist MD or APP · Rapid Response Team.
